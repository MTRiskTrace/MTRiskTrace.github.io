01 / Benchmark anatomy

A benchmark built for realistic context.

Cases span ordinary operational settings, two risk families, and tools that can read, communicate, or change state. The varied context matters because an agent may look safe early in a conversation and fail later.

DATASET SCOPE

Across 125 scenarios, the benchmark combines content and behavioral risks with five tool categories and six multi-turn attack strategies.

02 / The framework

From whether to when and why

Rather than scoring only the last response, the benchmark connects each safety judgment to the first observable risk event and its underlying capability failure.

THREE DIAGNOSES

Safety judgment establishes whether a trajectory is unsafe. Risk localization finds the first observable unsafe step. Capability diagnosis explains the failure through four safety dimensions.

03 / Construction & annotation

Build the case. Trace the failure.

Each case begins with a scenario, target risk, tool kit, and attack strategy. Human review then anchors the safety label to evidence in the trajectory rather than an assumed intent.

THE LABELS

04 / Evaluation

The same benchmark. Different failure paths.

Eleven models are evaluated across three agent harnesses. Attack success rate shows the outcome; the accompanying analyses locate risk, diagnose capability failures, compare tool categories, and measure agreement with human reviewers.

LOWER ASR IS BETTER

Under the common OpenClaw setting, Claude Sonnet 4.6 has the lowest overall attack success rate (8.55%); Llama 3.3-70B has the highest (87.18%).

05 / A trajectory in focus

The boundary was clear. Then it was abandoned.

A case study illustrates the value of tracing: an agent initially maintains an approval requirement, then performs a state-changing action in a later turn without valid authorization.

To evaluate an agent’s safety, we need to see the path it took—not only the response it left behind.
